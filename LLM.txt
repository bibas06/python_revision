Temprature variable in LLM
Technically, temperature is a parameter that controls the randomness or entropy of the model's output
by shaping the probability distribution of the next predicted token (word or word piece).

Here’s the step-by-step process:

Prediction: For the next word, the LLM calculates a probability score for every word in its vocabulary. This list of scores is called a "probability distribution."

Example: The model predicts the next word after "The sky is" could be:

blue (70% probability)

clear (15% probability)

spacious (5% probability)

the (4% probability)

green (1% probability)

...and so on for thousands of other words.

Applying Temperature: The temperature setting adjusts these probabilities before the model makes its final choice.

It does this by dividing the logits (the raw output scores before they are converted to probabilities) by the temperature value.

The Final Choice: The model then samples (picks) a word from this adjusted probability distribution.

You might also see these parameters, which work alongside temperature to control output:

Top-K: The model only considers the K most likely next words for selection. If K=3, it only chooses from the top 3 words (blue, clear, spacious from our example), ignoring all others.

Top-P (Nucleus Sampling): The model considers the smallest set of words whose combined probability exceeds P. If P=0.9, it would keep adding the most probable words until the sum of their probabilities is >= 0.9, then choose only from that set. This is more dynamic than Top-K.
